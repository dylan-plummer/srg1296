\begin{abstract}
We construct strongly regular graphs with parameters $(1296,560,244,240)$ and $(1296,592,268,272)$, for which no construction was previously known. We also construct the first vertex-transitive graphs with parameters $(1296,595,274,272)$, a parameter set realized independently by Kova\v{l}. All are Cayley graphs of $\Z_2^4\times\Z_3^4$, realized as the additive group of $\F_{16}\times\F_{81}$, whose connection sets are unions of orbits of a small group of field multiplications and a Galois map. We find $10$, $4$ and $4$ pairwise non-isomorphic graphs with the three parameter sets. The connection sets were found by an exact constraint-programming search for partial difference sets in products of finite fields, which extends Kova\v{l}'s search in $\F_4\times\F_{81}$ for $\srg(324,136,58,56)$. The three families are linked by spreads of Delsarte cliques. Some of the graphs with the first parameter set arise from an $\srg(1296,665,340,342)$ by deleting three pairwise orthogonal spreads; this $\srg(1296,665,340,342)$ is the graph of a symmetric Bush-type Hadamard matrix, which answers a question of Kova\v{l}. The graphs with the third parameter set arise by deleting two. None of the graphs with Latin square parameters is the graph of an orthogonal array. In one of the graphs with the first parameter set, every coset of $\F_{81}$ induces the Brouwer--Haemers graph $\srg(81,20,1,6)$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A strongly regular graph $\srg(v,k,\lambda,\mu)$ is a $k$-regular graph on $v$ vertices, neither complete nor edgeless, in which any two adjacent vertices have exactly $\lambda$ common neighbors and any two distinct non-adjacent vertices exactly $\mu$. Brouwer maintains an online table of feasible parameter sets~\cite{BrouwerTable}; see also the monograph of Brouwer and Van Maldeghem~\cite{BvM2022}. All three parameter sets of Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and~\ref{thm:595} were marked there as open when this work began.

A standard route to strongly regular graphs is through partial difference sets (PDS). If $D$ is a $(v,k,\lambda,\mu)$-PDS in a group $G$ with $D=D^{(-1)}$ and $1\notin D$, then the Cayley graph $\Cay(G,D)$ is an $\srg(v,k,\lambda,\mu)$~\cite[Proposition~1.1]{Ma1994}. Brady~\cite{Brady2025} turned this into a systematic computational program aimed at \emph{new} strongly regular graphs: a local search found PDSs with $62$ parameter sets in $1254$ non-isomorphic groups of order at most $147$, including the first constructions of $\srg(144,52,16,20)$ and $\srg(147,66,25,33)$.

Recently Kova\v{l}~\cite[Section~5]{KovalNew} realized the Latin square parameters $\LS_8(18)=(324,136,58,56)$ and the negative Latin square parameters $\NL_6(18)$ and $\NL_7(18)$ by PDSs in $\Z_2^2\times\Z_3^4$. One of these is a cyclotomic construction in the additive group of $\F_4\times\F_{81}$: the connection set is invariant under multiplication by the fourth roots of unity $\mu_4\le\F_{81}^*$, and each of its four slices $D_a$, $a\in\F_4$, is a union of cyclotomic classes of index~$20$ in~$\F_{81}$~\cite[Construction~5.4]{KovalNew}.

This paper replaces $\F_4$ by $\F_{16}$ and searches systematically over the symmetry groups that the product structure provides (Section~\ref{sec:search}). On $1296=36^2$ vertices this produces the following.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There exist strongly regular graphs with parameters
\[
 (1296,560,244,240)\ \ (\LS_{16}(36)) \qquad\text{and}\qquad (1296,592,268,272)\ \ (\NL_{16}(36)).
\]
There are at least $10$ pairwise non-isomorphic graphs with the first parameter set and at least $4$ with the second. All of them are vertex-transitive Cayley graphs of $\Z_2^4\times\Z_3^4$.
\end{theorem}

The parameters $(1296,595,274,272)=\LS_{17}(36)$ were realized independently by Kova\v{l}~\cite{KovalMultiOrbit}, with six graphs, none of them vertex-transitive. Our search gives the first vertex-transitive examples.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:595}
There are at least $4$ pairwise non-isomorphic vertex-transitive strongly regular graphs with parameters $(1296,595,274,272)$. They are Cayley graphs of $\Z_2^4\times\Z_3^4$, so their connection sets are abelian partial difference sets with these parameters, and none of them is isomorphic to a graph of~\cite{KovalMultiOrbit}.
\end{theorem}

The three families are related by deleting spreads of Delsarte cliques (Section~\ref{sec:spreads}). Two of the graphs with parameters $\LS_{16}(36)$ arise from a Cayley graph with parameters $\LS_{19}(36)$ by deleting three pairwise orthogonal spreads, and this Cayley graph is the graph of a symmetric Bush-type Hadamard matrix. This answers a question of Kova\v{l}~\cite[Section~4.10]{KovalMultiOrbit} (Proposition~\ref{prop:bush}). One of the two arises from an $\LS_{21}(36)$ Cayley graph by deleting five pairwise orthogonal spreads, the largest possible number in this group. The graphs of Theorem~\ref{thm:595} arise from $\LS_{19}(36)$ Cayley graphs by deleting two orthogonal spreads, as Kova\v{l}'s do.

\section{Preliminaries}

\subsection{Latin square type parameters}
The \emph{Latin square} and \emph{negative Latin square} parameters~\cite[Section~2]{Ma1994} are, for $n\ge2$ and $m\ge1$,
\begin{align*}
\LS_m(n)&=\bigl(n^2,\,m(n-1),\,n-2+(m-1)(m-2),\,m(m-1)\bigr),\\
\NL_m(n)&=\bigl(n^2,\,m(n+1),\,-n+m^2+3m,\,m(m+1)\bigr).
\end{align*}
For $m\le n$ and $m\le n-2$ respectively these are the parameters of graphs that are neither complete nor edgeless. An $\LS_m(n)$ graph has eigenvalues $n-m$ and $-m$; an $\NL_m(n)$ graph has eigenvalues $m$ and $m-n$. The graph of an orthogonal array $\mathrm{OA}(n,m)$ (equivalently, of $m-2$ mutually orthogonal Latin squares of order $n$) is an $\LS_m(n)$ graph, and every edge of it lies in a clique of size $n$. For $n=36$ only eight MOLS are known, so known orthogonal arrays give $\LS_m(36)$ graphs only for $m\le10$ (and their complements); Brouwer's table~\cite{BrouwerTable} lists $\mathrm{OA}(36,10)$ as known and $\mathrm{OA}(36,11)$ as open.

\subsection{Partial difference sets and characters}
Let $G$ be a finite group of order $v$. A $k$-subset $D\subseteq G$ is a $(v,k,\lambda,\mu)$-\emph{partial difference set} (PDS) if the products $gh^{-1}$ with $g,h\in D$, $g\ne h$, represent every non-identity element of $D$ exactly $\lambda$ times and every non-identity element outside $D$ exactly $\mu$ times; it is \emph{regular} if $1\notin D$ and $D=D^{(-1)}$~\cite[Section~1]{Ma1994}. From now on $G$ is abelian and written additively, so a regular PDS satisfies $0\notin D=-D$. Let $(v,k,\lambda,\mu)$ be feasible with integral eigenvalues $r>s$, the roots of $x^2-(\lambda-\mu)x-(k-\mu)$, and let $D\subseteq G\setminus\{0\}$ with $D=-D$ and $|D|=k$.

\begin{lemma}[{\cite[Corollary~3.3]{Ma1994}}]\label{lem:char}
$D$ is a $(v,k,\lambda,\mu)$-PDS if and only if $\chi(D)=\sum_{d\in D}\chi(d)\in\{r,s\}$ for every non-principal character $\chi$ of $G$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[{\cite[Theorem~4.1]{Ma1994}}]\label{lem:mult}
Let $D$ be a regular PDS in an abelian group $G$. If $(\lambda-\mu)^2+4(k-\mu)$ is a perfect square, then $tD=D$ for every integer $t$ coprime to $|G|$.
\end{lemma}
In particular, a PDS with integral eigenvalues is a union of orbits of the multiplier group $\{x\mapsto tx:\gcd(t,|G|)=1\}$. Any additional group of automorphisms that we impose on $D$ can therefore be taken to contain the multipliers, with no loss of generality.

\section{The search}\label{sec:search}

\subsection{Products of finite fields}
Let $G=\F_{q_1}\times\cdots\times\F_{q_\ell}$ (additive group) with $q_i=p_i^{e_i}$ and $v=\prod q_i$. Every $\boldsymbol\gamma=(\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_\ell)\in\prod\F_{q_i}^*$ acts on $G$ by $(a_i)\mapsto(\gamma_ia_i)$, and every $\boldsymbol\sigma=(\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_\ell)$ with $\sigma_i\in\mathrm{Gal}(\F_{q_i}/\F_{p_i})$ acts by $(a_i)\mapsto(\sigma_i(a_i))$. Both are group automorphisms. For a subgroup $S\le\prod\F_{q_i}^*$ and an optional Galois element $\boldsymbol\sigma$ we let
\[
K(S,\boldsymbol\sigma)=\bigl\langle\, S,\ \boldsymbol\sigma,\ \{x\mapsto tx:\gcd(t,v)=1\}\,\bigr\rangle\le\Aut(G).
\]
A \emph{model} is a pair $(S,\boldsymbol\sigma)$. Its solutions are the $K(S,\boldsymbol\sigma)$-invariant PDSs in $G$ with prescribed parameters. The models $(S,\boldsymbol\sigma)$ and $(S,\boldsymbol\sigma^{-1})$ give the same group. For $G=\F_{16}\times\F_{81}$ an integer $t$ coprime to $6$ acts as $(a,b)\mapsto(a,\pm b)$, so the multipliers contribute only $-1=(1,\beta^{40})$.

\subsection{An exact formulation}
Let $O_1,\dots,O_N$ be the orbits of $K=K(S,\boldsymbol\sigma)$ on $G$, and let $\Omega_1,\dots,\Omega_M$ be the orbits of the dual action of $K$ on the character group $\widehat G$. The value $\chi(O_i)$ depends only on the dual orbit of $\chi$. Because $K$ contains all multipliers, $\chi(O_i)$ is fixed by $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\exp G})/\mathbb{Q})$, so $c_{ji}=\chi_j(O_i)\in\Z$ for a representative $\chi_j\in\Omega_j$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:char}, $K$-invariant $(v,k,\lambda,\mu)$-PDSs correspond exactly to the $0/1$ solutions $(x,b)$ of
\begin{gather*}
\sum_i |O_i|\,x_i=k,\qquad x_{\{0\}}=0,\qquad \sum_j|\Omega_j|\,b_j=f,\\
\sum_i c_{ji}\,x_i=s+(r-s)\,b_j\quad(\Omega_j\ne\{1\}),
\end{gather*}
where $f$ is the multiplicity of the eigenvalue $r$; the equation $\sum_j|\Omega_j|\,b_j=f$ is implied by the others but helps the solver. We solve this integer program with the CP-SAT solver of OR-Tools~\cite{CPSAT}. Every solution is checked independently by the full identity $A^2=kI+\lambda A+\mu(J-I-A)$ for $A$ the adjacency matrix of $\Cay(G,D)$.

\subsection{Relation to Kova\v{l}'s search}
Kova\v{l}'s cyclotomic search on $\F_4\times\F_{81}$~\cite[Section~5.4]{KovalNew} is the analogue of this scheme for $(q_1,q_2)=(4,81)$, except that it also allows non-cyclic Galois parts. There $S$ runs over the $16$ subgroups of even order of the cyclic group $\F_4^*\times\F_{81}^*\cong C_{240}$, and the Galois part over the $8$ subgroups of $\mathrm{Gal}(\F_4/\F_2)\times\mathrm{Gal}(\F_{81}/\F_3)$. For $(q_1,q_2)=(16,81)$ the group $\F_{16}^*\times\F_{81}^*\cong C_{15}\times C_{80}$ is not cyclic, and we let $S$ run over all of its $80$ subgroups. Here $S$ need not have even order, because $-1$ is among the multipliers. $\boldsymbol\sigma$ runs over the trivial map and the $15$ maps $(a,b)\mapsto(a^{2^u},b^{3^w})$ with $(u,w)\ne(0,0)$. This gives $1280$ models. The $1251$ models with at most $200$ orbits were each tested against every parameter set $(1296,k,\lambda,\mu)$ with $k\le 648$ and integral eigenvalues that was open in Brouwer's table; complements cover the rest. Section~\ref{sec:graphs} presents the solutions, which have the three parameter sets of Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and~\ref{thm:595}. The same scheme was also run on other products of finite fields; the solver settings and the coverage of these searches are recorded in the data repository.

\section{The graphs}\label{sec:graphs}

Throughout, $\F_{16}=\F_2[x]/(x^4+x^3+1)$ and $\F_{81}=\F_3[y]/(y^4+y^3+2)$, with primitive elements $\alpha=x$ and $\beta=y$. Vertices are pairs $(a,b)\in\F_{16}\times\F_{81}$, and $(a,b)\sim(a',b')$ if and only if $(a-a',b-b')\in D$.

\begin{construction}[Graph I]\label{con:I}
Let
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_I=\{&(0,0),(0,2),(0,6),(0,11),(0,13),(0,14),(0,17),(0,18),(0,19),\\
&(1,3),(1,6),(1,7),(1,8),(1,10),(1,11),(1,13),(1,15),(1,19),\\
&(2,1),(2,2),(2,5),(2,9),(2,10),(2,11),(2,13),(2,16),(2,17)\}\subseteq\Z_3\times\Z_{20},
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\begin{multline*}
D_I=\{(0,\beta^{j}):j\equiv0\pmod 4\}\\
\cup\ \bigl\{(\alpha^i,\beta^j):0\le i<15,\ 0\le j<80,\ \bigl(i\bmod 3,\ (j-4\lfloor i/3\rfloor)\bmod 20\bigr)\in S_I\bigr\}.
\end{multline*}
Then $|D_I|=20+27\cdot20=560$. $D_I$ is invariant under $(a,b)\mapsto(\alpha^3a,\beta^4b)$, which has order $20$ and whose tenth power is $-1$, and under $(a,b)\mapsto(a^2,b^{27})$.
\end{construction}

\begin{construction}[Graph II]\label{con:II}
Let $J_a\subseteq\Z_{20}$ for $a\in\F_{16}$ be given by Table~\ref{tab:J}, and
\[
D_{II}=\{(a,\beta^j):a\in\F_{16},\ 0\le j<80,\ j\bmod 20\in J_a\}.
\]
Then $|D_{II}|=4\,(13+15\cdot9)=592$. $D_{II}$ is invariant under multiplication by $\mu_4=\langle\beta^{20}\rangle$ on the second coordinate and under $(a,b)\mapsto(a^2,b^3)$. Each slice $D_a=\{b:(a,b)\in D_{II}\}$ is a union of the cyclotomic classes $\beta^j\mu_4$ of index $20$. This is the shape of Kova\v{l}'s Construction~5.4 in~\cite{KovalNew}, with $\F_4$ replaced by $\F_{16}$.
\end{construction}

\begin{construction}[Graph III]\label{con:III}
Let $J'_a\subseteq\Z_{20}$ for $a\in\F_{16}$ be given by Table~\ref{tab:J}, and
\[
D_{III}=\{(a,\beta^j):a\in\F_{16},\ 0\le j<80,\ j\bmod 20\in J'_a\}\ \cup\ \bigl(\F_4^*\times\{0\}\bigr),
\]
where $\F_4^*=\{1,\alpha^5,\alpha^{10}\}$. Then $|D_{III}|=4\,(7+3\cdot11+12\cdot9)+3=595$. $D_{III}$ is invariant under multiplication by $\mu_4$ on the second coordinate and under $(a,b)\mapsto(a^2,b^3)$. The three slices with $|J'_a|=11$ are those with $a\in\F_4^*$.
\end{construction}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:graphs}
$D_I$, $D_{II}$ and $D_{III}$ are partial difference sets in $(\F_{16}\times\F_{81},+)\cong\Z_2^4\times\Z_3^4$ with parameters $(1296,560,244,240)$, $(1296,592,268,272)$ and $(1296,595,274,272)$. Consequently the Cayley graphs $\Gamma_I$, $\Gamma_{II}$, $\Gamma_{III}$ of $\F_{16}\times\F_{81}$ with these connection sets are strongly regular, with spectra $560^1\,20^{560}\,(-16)^{735}$, $592^1\,16^{703}\,(-20)^{592}$ and $595^1\,19^{595}\,(-17)^{700}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All three sets satisfy $D=-D$ and $0\notin D$. The identity $A^2=kI+\lambda A+\mu(J-I-A)$ was verified exhaustively, by counting the common neighbors of all $\binom{1296}{2}$ vertex pairs of $\Cay(G,D)$ (Section~\ref{sec:verification}). As a consistency check, all $1295$ non-principal character values were computed numerically. They take exactly the two values $r,s$ allowed by Lemma~\ref{lem:char}, with the multiplicities of the stated spectrum.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Unlike Kova\v{l}'s Construction~5.4, which is proved by hand in~\cite[Section~5.3]{KovalNew}, these proofs are computational. A proof in that style should be possible, since every character value of $D_I$, $D_{II}$ and $D_{III}$ is a sum of Gauss periods of index $20$ of $\F_{81}$ with signs $\pm1$ given by additive characters of $\F_{16}$.
\end{remark}

\section{Properties}\label{sec:properties}

\subsection{Clique numbers}
In the graph of an $\mathrm{OA}(36,m)$ every edge lies in a $36$-clique, which attains the Delsarte bound $1-k/s=36$ on the size of a clique~\cite{BvM2022}. We computed the clique numbers $\omega$ of all eighteen graphs exactly (Table~\ref{tab:classes}). Since $\Gamma$ is vertex-transitive, a maximum clique may be taken to contain $(0,0)$ and an edge $\{(0,0),x\}$, so $\omega(\Gamma)=2+\max_x\omega(\Gamma(0,0)\cap\Gamma(x))$. Here $x$ runs over representatives of the orbits of the known automorphisms fixing $(0,0)$ on its neighbors. Each common neighborhood has $\lambda$ vertices, and its clique number was computed exactly with Cliquer~\cite{Cliquer} (through igraph). A clique of size $\omega$ was exhibited in each case, and the values for I.1 and I.8 were confirmed with a second exact solver (networkx). The clique numbers are $12$, $14$ or $15$ for the graphs I, $12$ or $13$ for the graphs II and $24$ for the graphs III. They are far below the Delsarte bounds, which are $36$ for I and III and $\lfloor 1+592/20\rfloor=30$ for II. In particular none of the graphs I and III is the graph of an orthogonal array, and they say nothing about the existence of $14$ or $15$ MOLS of order $36$.

\subsection{Ring structure}
Let $R_a=\{a\}\times\F_{81}$ for $a\in\F_{16}$; we call these sets \emph{rings}. Since they are the cosets of a subgroup, $\{R_a\}$ is an equitable partition of every Cayley graph of $G$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rings}
In $\Gamma_I$ each ring induces the Brouwer--Haemers graph $\srg(81,20,1,6)$~\cite{BrouwerHaemers1992}, namely the cyclotomic graph $\Cay(\F_{81},\langle\beta^4\rangle)$, and every vertex has exactly $36$ neighbors in each of the other $15$ rings. In $\Gamma_{II}$ each ring induces a $52$-regular graph that is not strongly regular, and every vertex has exactly $36$ neighbors in each other ring. In $\Gamma_{III}$ each ring induces a $28$-regular graph that is not strongly regular. A vertex of $R_a$ has $45$ neighbors in each of the three rings $R_{a+c}$, $c\in\F_4^*$, and $36$ in each of the other twelve rings.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The subgraph induced on $R_a$ is $\Cay(\F_{81},D\cap(\{0\}\times\F_{81}))$, and $D_I\cap(\{0\}\times\F_{81})=\{0\}\times\langle\beta^4\rangle$. This cyclotomic graph is an $\srg(81,20,1,6)$, as a direct count shows, and an $\srg(81,20,1,6)$ is unique~\cite{BrouwerHaemers1992}, so it is the Brouwer--Haemers graph. The remaining counts are $|D\cap(\{a\}\times\F_{81})|$ for $a\neq0$, read off from the constructions. For $\Gamma_{II}$, adjacent pairs in a ring have $31$ or $37$ common neighbors inside the ring, and for $\Gamma_{III}$ they have $1$, $7$ or $13$.
\end{proof}

The quotient matrix of the partition $\{R_a\}$ is $cI+36(J-I)$ with $c=20$ for $\Gamma_I$ and $c=52$ for $\Gamma_{II}$. Its eigenvalues are $c+15\cdot36=k$ and $c-36$, which equals $-16$ for $\Gamma_I$ and $16$ for $\Gamma_{II}$, as they must be. For $\Gamma_{III}$ the quotient matrix is not of this form, because the subfield $\F_4$ singles out three rings, and it has eigenvalues $595$, $19$ and $-17$. Figure~\ref{fig:rings} illustrates the ring structure, and its right-hand panels zoom into a single ring. An interactive visualization of all eighteen graphs, including their adjacency matrices, is in the data repository. The ring graphs of $\Gamma_{II}$ show that strong regularity of $\Gamma$ does not require strongly regular rings: two non-adjacent vertices of the same ring have $26$, $32$ or $38$ common neighbors inside the ring (adjacent ones $31$ or $37$), and the other $15$ rings contribute exactly the complementary amount.

\subsection{Spread structure}\label{sec:spreads}
Following Haemers and Tonchev~\cite{HaemersTonchev1996}, a \emph{spread} of a strongly regular graph is a partition of its vertex set into Delsarte cliques, that is, cliques of size $1-k/s$. Two spreads are \emph{orthogonal} if every clique of one meets every clique of the other in exactly one vertex. Kova\v{l}~\cite[Lemma~4.1]{KovalMultiOrbit}, building on~\cite[Proposition~4.1]{HaemersTonchev1996}, showed the following: if $\mathcal S_1,\dots,\mathcal S_j$ are pairwise orthogonal spreads of a primitive strongly regular graph with the parameters of $\LS_m(n)$, $j<m$, then deleting the edges inside all their cliques leaves a strongly regular graph with the parameters of $\LS_{m-j}(n)$. Kova\v{l} constructed $\LS_{n/2-1}(n)$ graphs this way, by deleting two orthogonal spreads from $\LS_{n/2+1}(n)$ graphs coming from Bush-type Hadamard matrices, and asked whether a third orthogonal spread can be deleted, which for $n=36$ would give the parameters $\LS_{16}(36)=(1296,560,244,240)$ of $\Gamma_I$~\cite[Section~4.10]{KovalMultiOrbit}.

In a Cayley graph $\Cay(G,E)$, a subgroup $U$ of order $36$ with $U\setminus\{0\}\subseteq E$ makes the cosets of $U$ cliques of size $36$. For an $\LS_m(36)$ graph these are Delsarte cliques, since $1-k/s=1+35m/m=36$. So the cosets of $U$ form a spread, and the spreads of two such subgroups $U,V$ are orthogonal exactly when $U\cap V=\{0\}$. Deleting the spread of $U$ replaces $E$ by $E\setminus U$. Conversely, we call a subgroup $U$ of order $36$ \emph{free} for $D$ if $U\cap D=\emptyset$; adding $U^*=U\setminus\{0\}$ to $D$ may or may not give a PDS.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:spreads}
Free subgroups exist only for the graphs I.6, I.10 and III.1--III.4, whose connection sets $D$ are of type $\LS_m(36)$ with $m=16$ and $m=17$. For these graphs, if $U_1,\dots,U_j$ are free subgroups with pairwise intersection $\{0\}$, then $D\cup U_1^*\cup\dots\cup U_j^*$ is an $\LS_{m+j}(36)$ PDS. The free subgroups are as follows (Figure~\ref{fig:spreads}(b)).
\begin{enumerate}
\item I.6 has $5$ free subgroups, and they intersect pairwise in $\{0\}$. So $D_{\mathrm{I.6}}\cup U_1^*\cup\dots\cup U_5^*$ is an $\LS_{21}(36)$ PDS, and $\Gamma_{\mathrm{I.6}}$ arises from an $\LS_{21}(36)$ Cayley graph by deleting five pairwise orthogonal spreads. By the theorem of Bailey and Jungnickel (Section~\ref{sec:known}), five is the largest possible number of subgroups of order $36$ of $G$ that intersect pairwise in $\{0\}$.
\item I.10 has $8$ free subgroups, with $18$ pairs, $8$ triples and one set of four that intersect pairwise in $\{0\}$. So $\Gamma_{\mathrm{I.10}}$ arises from an $\LS_{20}(36)$ Cayley graph by deleting four pairwise orthogonal spreads.
\item III.1 and III.3 have $4$ free subgroups each, and they intersect pairwise in $\{0\}$. III.2 and III.4 have $8$ each, with $12$ pairs, $4$ triples and one set of four that intersect pairwise in $\{0\}$. So every graph III arises from an $\LS_{21}(36)$ Cayley graph by deleting four pairwise orthogonal spreads.
\end{enumerate}
In particular $\Gamma_{\mathrm{I.6}}$ and $\Gamma_{\mathrm{I.10}}$ arise from an $\LS_{19}(36)$ Cayley graph by deleting three pairwise orthogonal spreads, in $10$ and $8$ ways, and every graph III arises from an $\LS_{19}(36)$ Cayley graph by deleting two.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By exhaustive computation over the $35\cdot130$ subgroups of order $36$, which are the products of a subgroup of order $4$ of $\F_{16}$ (there are $35$) and a subgroup of order $9$ of $\F_{81}$ (there are $130$). Every set of free subgroups with pairwise trivial intersection was tested with the character criterion of Lemma~\ref{lem:char}. For one triple of each of I.6 and I.10, strong regularity of all four Cayley graphs $\Cay(G,D)$, $\Cay(G,D\cup U^*)$, $\Cay(G,D\cup U^*\cup V^*)$ and $\Cay(G,D\cup U^*\cup V^*\cup W^*)$ was also checked by counting common neighbors.
\end{proof}





Kova\v{l}~\cite[Theorem~4.3]{KovalMultiOrbit} obtains $\LS_{n/2+1}(n)$ graphs $\Gamma_0$ from symmetric Hadamard matrices $K$ of order $n^2$ of Bush type whose blocks have unit diagonal: rows and columns are indexed by $[n]\times[n]$, the diagonal blocks are $J_n$, the other blocks $K_{ac}$ satisfy $K_{ac}J_n=J_nK_{ac}=0$, and $K_{(a,b),(c,b)}=1$. The block rows and the block columns of $\Gamma_0$ are two orthogonal spreads, and deleting both gives an $\LS_{n/2-1}(n)$ graph $\Gamma_K$. He asked whether some $\Gamma_0$ has a third spread orthogonal to both~\cite[Section~4.10]{KovalMultiOrbit}. The Cayley graphs of Proposition~\ref{prop:spreads} answer this question positively for $n=36$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bush}
Let $E$ be an $\LS_{19}(36)$ PDS in $G$, and let $U,V$ be subgroups of order $36$ with $U\cap V=\{0\}$ and $U^*\cup V^*\subseteq E$. Index the vertices $g\in G$ by the pair $(g+U,g+V)$ of cosets, and let $K=2(A+I)-J$, where $A$ is the adjacency matrix of $\Cay(G,E)$. Then $K$ is a symmetric Hadamard matrix of Bush type whose blocks have unit diagonal, $\Cay(G,E)$ is its graph $\Gamma_0$, and $\Cay(G,E\setminus(U\cup V))$ is its graph $\Gamma_K$. If moreover $W$ is a subgroup of order $36$ with $W^*\subseteq E$ and $W\cap U=W\cap V=\{0\}$, then the cosets of $W$ form a third spread of $\Gamma_0$, orthogonal to the block rows and the block columns.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $G=U\oplus V$, each $U$-coset meets each $V$-coset in exactly one vertex, so the indexing is a bijection. $\Cay(G,E)$ is an $\srg(1296,665,340,342)$ with eigenvalues $665$, $17$ and $-19$, so $K$ is a symmetric $\pm1$ matrix with eigenvalues $36$ and $\pm36$, and $K^2=1296I$. The diagonal blocks are $J$ because the $U$-cosets are cliques, and $K_{(a,b),(c,b)}=1$ because the $V$-cosets are cliques. A vertex outside a Delsarte clique of size $36$ has exactly $\mu/(-s)=342/19=18$ neighbors in it~\cite{BvM2022}, so every row of an off-diagonal block has $18$ entries $1$ and $18$ entries $-1$; hence $K_{ac}J=0$, and $JK_{ac}=0$ by symmetry. The adjacency matrix of $\Cay(G,E)$ is $\frac12(J+K)-I$, which is $\Gamma_0$, and deleting the spreads of $U$ and $V$ gives $\Gamma_K$. Finally, $W\cap U=W\cap V=\{0\}$ means that every $W$-coset meets every block row and every block column in one vertex, and $W^*\subseteq E$ makes the $W$-cosets cliques.
\end{proof}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:spreads}, Proposition~\ref{prop:bush} applies to every graph III, with $E=D\cup U^*\cup V^*$ for a pair of orthogonal free subgroups, so the graphs III are graphs $\Gamma_K$ of Bush-type Hadamard matrices developed over $G$, like Kova\v{l}'s $\LS_{17}(36)$ graphs. For I.6 and I.10, with $E=D\cup U^*\cup V^*\cup W^*$, it also gives the third spread. All Bush-type and spread statements above were also checked directly on the matrices.

\subsection{Relation to known constructions}\label{sec:known}
We checked the constructions of Latin square and negative Latin square type PDSs that could apply to abelian groups of order $1296=2^4\cdot3^4$. None of the constructions we checked gives any of our three parameter sets.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Partial congruence partitions.} In an abelian group of order $36^2$, a set of subgroups of order $36$ that intersect pairwise trivially has at most $\min(2^2+1,3^2+1)=5$ members, by a theorem of Bailey and Jungnickel~\cite[Theorem~2.4]{Ma1994}. So these give $\LS_m(36)$ only for $m\le5$.
\item \emph{Product constructions.} The product theorems for packings of Latin square and negative Latin square type PDSs~\cite[Theorems 3.15, 6.8, Corollaries 3.17, 6.10]{JedwabLi2021}, including Polhill's product construction~\cite{Polhill2008}, combine packings in groups $G_1$, $G_2$ of orders $n_1^2$, $n_2^2$ with $n_1n_2=n$. They give $\LS_m(n)$ or $\NL_m(n)$ only when $m$ is a multiple of $n/t$ with $t>1$ dividing $\gcd(n_1,n_2)$. For $n=36$ we have $\gcd(n_1,n_2)\le 6$, so $m$ is a multiple of $6$. Our values $m=16$ ($\LS$ and $\NL$) and $m=17$ ($\LS$) are not, and neither are the complementary values $21$, $19$ and $20$.
\item \emph{Momihara's families}~\cite[Theorems 1.4, 1.5]{Momihara2021} live in products of elementary abelian groups of orders $q_i^4$ with $2m\mid q_i+1$ for all $i$, for an integer $m$ (not the Latin square index). For $1296=2^4\cdot3^4$ this would require $2m\mid 3$ and $2m\mid 4$, which is impossible.
\item \emph{Cyclotomy over products of finite fields.} The standard cyclotomy of Fern\'andez-Alcober, Kwashira and Mart\'\i nez~\cite{FKM2010} on $\F_{16}\times\F_{81}$ has order $e\mid\gcd(15,80)=5$. Its PDSs are unions of orbits of a subgroup $H\le\F_{16}^*\times\F_{81}^*$ of index $e$. For $e=1$ the orbits have sizes $1,15,80,1200$, so no union has size $560$, $592$ or $595$. For $e=5$ there are four choices of $H$, the orbits have sizes $1$, $15$, $80$ and $240$, and an exhaustive check shows that none of the $40$ unions of $H$-orbits of size $560$ is a PDS; no union of $H$-orbits has size $592$ or $595$. So this cyclotomy gives none of our parameter sets. Independently, our connection sets are not of this form: their multiplicative stabilizers have order at most $20$ (Table~\ref{tab:classes}), whereas a union of $H$-orbits is invariant under $H$, which has order $1200/e\ge240$. Ott's generalizations~\cite{Ott2016b,Ott2016a} consider unions of cosets of $U_\chi=\{(x_1,x_2):\chi_1(x_1)\chi_2(x_2)=1\}$, together with $U_\chi$-orbits of non-units, for multiplicative characters $\chi_i$ of order $d\mid q_i-1$. As Ott notes~\cite[Section~1]{Ott2016b}, these are exactly the subgroups of~\cite{FKM2010}. So here $d\mid5$, and the same two arguments exclude them. The new family of~\cite[Theorem~3]{Ott2016a} lives in groups of prime-power order $q^{2a+1}$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Verification}\label{sec:verification}
Each claimed graph was checked in three independent ways, and all checks pass.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A standalone program (plain Python, no external packages, no code shared with the search) builds $\Cay(G,D)$ with its own arithmetic in $\F_{16}$ and $\F_{81}$. It checks $D=-D$, $0\notin D$, the degree, and the number of common neighbors of all $839{,}160$ vertex pairs. It is run on Constructions~\ref{con:I}--\ref{con:III} exactly as printed above, and on the connection sets of all eighteen graphs.
\item Kova\v{l}'s independent verifier \texttt{verify\_srg.py}, from the data accompanying~\cite{KovalNew}, was run on the graph6 files of all eighteen graphs.
\item The spectra were computed numerically, and isomorphism classes and automorphism groups were computed with nauty~\cite{McKayPiperno2014}.
\end{enumerate}
